# Someone sent you a link

*No technical knowledge needed. You do not have to install anything.*

A teammate is working with an AI assistant, and they have opened it up so you
can be in there with them. This page is everything you need.

## What this actually is

Think of it as a group chat where one of the participants is an AI — except the
AI only listens when the group agrees it should.

Your teammate has the AI open on their computer. It is helping them with their
work. Normally only they can talk to it and only they can see what it says.

The link they sent you changes that. You can now:

- **See everything** the AI says, as it says it
- **Suggest** what to ask it next
- **Agree or disagree** with what other people suggest

Nothing gets sent to the AI until enough people say yes. That is the whole idea.

## Getting in

**Click the link.** A page opens.

**Type your name** in the box and click **Join in the browser**.

That is it. You are in. Nothing to install, no account, no password.

> You may see a line of computer code above the name box, labelled "in your
> terminal". That is for programmers who want a fancier version. **Ignore it.**
> The browser version does everything the same.

## Using it

The page has three parts:

- **The middle** is the conversation. Everything the AI says appears here.
- **The top** shows who else is in the room.
- **The bottom** is where you type.

### Suggesting something

Type it in the box at the bottom and press Enter.

**Important:** what you type does *not* go to the AI. It goes to your teammates
as a suggestion, with a number on it, like `#4`. Everyone in the room sees it
and decides together whether to send it.

That surprises people the first time. It is deliberate — it is the reason the
tool exists.

### Voting

When someone suggests something, you will see **Approve** and **Veto** buttons.

- **Approve** — you are happy for this to be sent to the AI
- **Veto** — you are not. You can type a reason, and everyone sees it

There is often a countdown. If nobody objects before it runs out, the
suggestion goes through. So if you disagree, say so rather than staying quiet.

### Talking without involving the AI

Sometimes you want to say something to the *people*, not to the AI. Type:

```
/say can we check with design first?
```

Anything starting with `/say` goes to your teammates only. The AI never sees it.

## The four things you can type

| Type this | What happens |
|---|---|
| anything | suggests it to the room |
| `/y` | agrees with the newest suggestion |
| `/n too risky` | disagrees, and says why |
| `/say hello` | talks to your teammates only |

The buttons do the same as `/y` and `/n`, so you can just click.

## Questions people ask

**Does this cost me anything?** No. It runs on your teammate's account.

**Can I break something?** Not by yourself. Anything that changes their work has
to be agreed by the group first. And you can only suggest — you are never the
one who presses go alone.

**Can everyone see what I type?** Yes, everyone in the room. Nothing is private.
It is also written to a log file on your teammate's computer, including who
agreed to what. That is on purpose — it is a record of how the group decided.

**Do I need to keep the tab open?** Yes, while you want to take part. If you
close it, you leave the room. Clicking the link again brings you back.

**What if I just want to watch?** Tell whoever invited you — they can send you a
read-only link, where you see everything but do not vote.

**Is the link private?** Very. **Do not forward it or post it anywhere public.**
Anyone who has that link can get into the room and read everything. Treat it
like a password.

**Something looks broken.** If the page says it cannot encrypt the connection,
tell the person who invited you — they need to set it up slightly differently.
It is their end, not yours.

## That is genuinely everything

Click the link, type your name, read along, and vote when something comes up.

## Want to run one yourself

Ask the coding agent you already use — Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini, and the
rest — to share a session. It prints a link. You send that link the same way
someone sent you this one.

That is the loop: every room creates the next host.
